\begin{abstract}
We study online humanoid operation with frontier multimodal models
and report results obtained in the last few weeks following their release.
AHOPPA connects model decisions to the fixed SONIC controller on a simulated Unitree G1.
The model uses cameras, a static map and a revisable plan to inspect four
cabinet drawers, push open drawers shut by hand and return home. It chooses
routes and generates numerical head and wrist targets with locomotion commands.
Physics continues during inference. A simulator evaluator checks completion.
Across 64 scheduled episodes
with four models at low and high reasoning effort, GPT-6 Astra completed
12 of 14 task attempts, taking 615 to 1492\,s. These episodes show
extended visual inspection, contact and return-home behavior through a fixed
controller without a task skill library. Falls, timeouts and incorrect
completion claims expose limits of this capability in simulation.
\end{abstract}
\keywords{Humanoid control, multimodal agents, online evaluation}

\section{Online humanoid operation}
Extended tasks require a humanoid to decide where to look, how to move and
when to stop. We test frontier models on a cabinet inspection task
using visual feedback and numerical SONIC references~\citep{sonic2026}.

Prior work grounds skills~\citep{saycan2022}, generates code~\citep{codeaspolicies2022},
uses feedback~\citep{innermonologue2022}, builds value maps~\citep{voxposer2023}
or scene-graph plans~\citep{sayplan2023}, selects visual actions~\citep{pivot2024},
uses keypoint constraints~\citep{rekep2025} or learns action tokens~\citep{rt22023}.
Embodied First Exam tests coding agents in continuous time~\citep{embodiedfirstexam2026}.
Drawer closing is established~\citep{rlbench2020}. Humanoids sequence
skills~\citep{homebody2026,promptplanperform2024} or generate policy code~\citep{choi2026hugo}.


\section{Task, interface and evaluation}
\textbf{Scene.} Newton physics~\citep{newton2025} in Isaac
Lab~\citep{isaaclab2025} runs at 200\,Hz with native SONIC at 50\,Hz. Four NVIDIA
Sektion cabinets from Isaac Lab's cabinet task~\citep{isaaclab2026cabinet}
stand in NVIDIA's Simple Room~\citep{nvidia2026warehouseassets,nvidia2026assetlicense}:
A and B on the far wall behind a 2.1\,m block of SimReady storage
lockers~\citep{nvidia2026simready}, C and D behind home, so no drawer front is
visible from the start. Each top drawer is a passive slide that closes only
through contact, with 5\,N dry friction and critical damping at its stop. The
robot stands on Isaac Lab's flat ground collider beneath the room's visual
floor. Eight conditions
open none, one or two drawers (0.20\,m) under an identical map and instruction.

\textbf{Interface.} The model emits four head/wrist pose keyframes with SONIC's
walking and posture commands, and binary Dex3 hand presets. It chooses route,
speed, facing, reach and timing; SONIC supplies gait, balance and tracking.
The interface holds facing commands within 15$^\circ$ of the measured heading
during turns, following the released gamepad.
There is no drawer-closing, navigation or inspection skill.
Each decision receives onboard RGB-D rendered from the measured torso pose,
a fixed external RGB view, robot-only kinematics, depth queries, the facility
map, its plan and recent action feedback. Simulator positions, drawer joints,
contacts and evaluator state remain private to the simulator.

\textbf{Scoring.} A drawer is closed when within 2\,cm of closed and still for
1\,s. Filtered normal-force sensors separate hand pushes from torso or other
contact. Success requires every drawer closed, a supported standing hold within 25\,cm of home, and the model's completion
declaration. Physical completion without a declaration, disturbed closed
drawers, hand attribution, falls, timeouts and infrastructure errors are
reported separately, as are controls with no open drawer.

\textbf{Protocol.} After floor and facing pilots, we froze
scene, cameras, map, prompts, evaluator, an 1800\,s budget and source, then ran
one seeded episode per condition for each of GPT-6.1 Sol, GPT-6 Astra, Claude
Opus 5.5 and Gemini 4 Argon at low and high reasoning effort, four episodes at
a time with pinned host placement; all starts are retained.

All 64 scheduled starts are retained (Table~\ref{tab:results},
Fig.~\ref{fig:episode}). Every one of the 43 closures was by hand and no
initially closed drawer was disturbed. Astra completed 6/7 episodes at both
efforts (615 to 1492\,s), usually checking every drawer, including the two behind
the lockers. Sol completed 4/7 and 3/7, Opus 2/7 and 3/7, and Gemini none; high
effort lengthened decisions (median 21 to 130\,s versus 9 to 28\,s) without
consistent gains, and eight episodes ran out of time. Fifteen of 51 completed
episodes starting with open drawers ended in falls, one at start-up before any model command;
four declarations were made without the physical criterion.

\textbf{Timing.} Median peak physics lag was 192\,ms; 17 of 58 completed
episodes, including 7 successes, met the 100\,ms limit. Falls rarely followed
lag spikes. The results include episodes that exceeded this timing limit.

\textbf{Limits.} We study one simulated layout, one drawer interaction and one episode per
model, effort and condition. Friction, damping and ground support are modeling
choices. Reliability, capability gains and transfer require repeated trials,
older-model baselines and hardware experiments.
