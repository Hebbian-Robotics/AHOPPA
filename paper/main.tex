\begin{abstract}
We study online humanoid operation with frontier multimodal models
and report results obtained in the last few weeks following their release.
AHOPPA connects model decisions to the fixed SONIC controller on a simulated Unitree G1.
The model uses cameras, a map and a revisable plan to inspect four drawers,
push open drawers shut by hand and return home through numerical head/wrist
and locomotion commands. Physics continues during inference; a simulator
evaluator checks completion.
Across 64 scheduled episodes
with four models at low and high reasoning effort, GPT-6 Astra completed
12 of 14 task attempts, taking 615 to 1492\,s. The episodes show visual inspection, contact and return-home behavior without a task
skill library. Falls, timeouts and incorrect completion claims expose limits
in simulation.
\end{abstract}
\keywords{Humanoid control, multimodal agents, online evaluation}

\section{Online humanoid operation}
We test whether frontier models can inspect cabinets, navigate, make contact
and decide when to stop through visual feedback and numerical SONIC
references~\citep{sonic2026}.

Prior work uses grounded skills~\citep{saycan2022,homebody2026,promptplanperform2024},
code~\citep{codeaspolicies2022,choi2026hugo}, feedback~\citep{innermonologue2022,pivot2024},
spatial representations~\citep{voxposer2023,sayplan2023,rekep2025} or learned
action tokens~\citep{rt22023}.

\section{Task, interface and evaluation}
\textbf{Scene.} Newton~\citep{newton2025} in Isaac Lab~\citep{isaaclab2025}
runs at 200\,Hz with SONIC at 50\,Hz. Four Sektion
cabinets~\citep{isaaclab2026cabinet} stand in NVIDIA's Simple
Room~\citep{nvidia2026warehouseassets,nvidia2026assetlicense}: A and B behind
2.1\,m lockers~\citep{nvidia2026simready}, C and D behind home. No drawer front
is visible initially. Passive drawers close through contact, with 5\,N dry
friction and critically damped stops; the robot uses a flat ground collider.
Eight conditions open zero, one or two drawers by 0.20\,m under the same map
and instruction.

\textbf{Interface.} Four head/wrist keyframes, walking/posture commands and
binary hand presets drive SONIC (Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}); facing stays within
15$^\circ$ of measured heading during turns. Decisions receive onboard RGB-D,
external RGB, robot kinematics, depth queries, map, plan and feedback. Drawer
joints, contacts and scores remain private; no task skills are provided.

\textbf{Scoring.} Closed means within 2\,cm of shut and still for 1\,s.
Force sensors distinguish hand pushes from other contact. Success requires all
drawers closed, a supported standing hold within 25\,cm of home and the model's
completion declaration. We separately record physical completion, disturbed
drawers, contact attribution, falls, timeouts, infrastructure errors and
all-closed controls.

\textbf{Protocol.} After pilots, we froze the setup, prompts, evaluator, source
and 1800\,s budget. GPT-6.1 Sol, GPT-6 Astra, Claude Opus 5.5 and Gemini 4
Argon each ran one seeded episode per condition at low and high effort, four
at a time with pinned host placement.

Table~\ref{tab:results} retains all 64 starts. All 43 closures were by hand; no
initially closed drawer was disturbed. Astra succeeded in 6/7 tasks at each
effort (615 to 1492\,s), usually checking every drawer. Sol succeeded in 4/7 and
3/7, Opus 2/7 and 3/7, Gemini none. High effort lengthened decisions without
consistent gains; eight episodes timed out. Fifteen of 51 completed task starts
ended in falls, one before any model command; four declarations lacked physical
completion.
In this sweep, Astra high cost less despite more output tokens because cached
reads covered 50\% of its input, versus 29\% at low.

\textbf{Timing.} Median peak physics lag was 192\,ms. Only 17/58 completed
episodes, including 7 successes, met the 100\,ms limit; the results retain
timing-limited episodes. Falls rarely followed lag spikes.

\textbf{Limits.} One simulated layout, drawer interaction and trial per
model/effort/condition limit generalization. Friction, damping and ground support
are modeling choices. Reliability, gains and transfer need repeated trials,
older-model baselines and hardware experiments.
